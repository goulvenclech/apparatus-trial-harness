# Case B, peopling of the Americas: answer key v1

Evidence cut-off 8 November 2026.

Why this case. A second discipline, a key independent of the apparatus's own sources, firm pairs at both ends with a contested middle, a status spread across many reviews none of which ranks every programme, and three twists later than most training data (Holliday 2025, the Monte Verde dispute of 2026, Davis & Madsen 2026).

## Individuation

Programmes are individuated as distinct hypotheses about the route and the timing of the first peopling south of the ice sheets. Each has a hard core of its own (a route, a date, a founding population) and a positive heuristic (where and how to look for sites or signals). Programmes (c), (d) and (e1) are compatible, so the key grades the solidity of each, not its probability of being the whole answer. A run that treats them as components of one synthesis is not penalised, provided it grades each.

Sites are evidence, not programmes. A run that ranks Monte Verde, White Sands or Cooper's Ferry as programmes makes a level error, scored under fabrication.

Boundary calls. Programme (d) is graded on its core, a phase of isolation and genetic structuring, and its location (Beringia, the Arctic plateau, east of Baikal) is a protective-belt dispute. Monte Verde II is coded contested since March 2026, neither refuted nor confirmed. Hartley Mammoth and Santa Elina are not evaluated, not refuted.

## Programmes and expected verdicts

| # | Programme | Verdict | Principal pathology | Grounds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | (d) Beringian standstill | solid | none (mono-method is the risk to name) | An isolation and structuring phase of the founding population, about 25 to 20 ka, reproduced by mtDNA, modern and ancient genomes (Llamas 2016; Moreno-Mayar 2018; Willerslev & Meltzer 2021). Location unresolved and duration revised down to 2.4 to 9 kyr (Potter et al. 2026). No archaeological signature. |
| 2 | (c) Pacific coastal route | defensible | none (untestability and alternative-blindness are the risks to name) | Coastal corridor deglaciated by 17.7 to 16 ka (Lesnek 2018), inland anchor at Cooper's Ferry/Nipéhe about 16 ka (Davis 2019; Davis & Madsen 2026). Preferred largely by elimination of the corridor, with no coastal site older than about 14 ka (Gauvreau 2023; Praetorius 2023) and submerged shorelines unexplored (Braje 2017). Monte Verde II contested since March 2026 (Surovell 2026; Dillehay 2026). Standing rests partly on elimination over a well-searched space, so defensible at most. |
| 3 | (e1) LGM-age occupation, the White Sands horizon at 21 to 23 ka | defensible | none (mono-method is the risk to name: tracks, no artefacts) | Seeds, pollen, OSL and lake mud converge, 55 radiocarbon dates from three laboratories (Bennett 2021; Pigati 2023; Holliday 2025); the reservoir and stratigraphy critiques (Rhode 2024) are answered. No artefacts, genomic diversification no earlier than about 21 ka, rated moderately contested (Potter et al. 2026). The age is robust, its implications are not. |
| 4 | (b) Interior corridor, pre-Clovis entry | fragile | ad-hocness | Rejection triangulated by sedaDNA, bison phylogeography and 10Be exposure ages, full opening at 13.8 ± 0.5 ka (Pedersen 2016; Heintzman 2016; Clark 2022). Survives only as an untested pre-LGM variant, entry before the 27 to 25 ka coalescence (Praetorius 2023), while the coastal rival gains ground. |
| 5 | (e2) Claims at 30 ka or older (Chiquihuite, Pedra Furada, Cerutti) | fragile | ad-hocness (insularity secondary) | Artefact status unresolved at every site (Chatters 2022 against Ardelean 2020; Ferraro 2018 against Holen 2017; the Boëda 2014 debate), no diagnostic tools, all rated most contested (Potter et al. 2026). Forty years of claims without an accepted site. |
| 6 | (a) Clovis-first, including its late-arrival variant | fragile | ad-hocness (lock-in in the historical record) | Core contradicted by three independent lines: the corridor viable only from 13.8 to 12.6 ka, the Anzick genome placing Clovis in an already diversified lineage (Rasmussen 2014), and dated occupations at 16 to 14.5 ka (Davis 2019; Waters 2019). Survives as a late-arrival variant that contests each site in turn (Surovell 2022, 2026). Strict form unsupported even by sceptics (Potter et al. 2022). The 1964 to 1997 resistance is the field's canonical lock-in episode (Meltzer 2009). |
| 7 | (f) Solutrean | fragile | insularity (overfit secondary) | Several thousand years between the end of the Solutrean and Clovis, convergent technology, no transitional site (Straus 2000; O'Brien 2014). No European Palaeolithic ancestry in Anzick (Rasmussen 2014). No peer-reviewed defence found for 2024 to 2026. |

No entry carries the (stagnant) tag. In every weak programme a rival is gaining ground, which fails Branahl's second criterion, so the weak programmes are degenerating, not stagnant.

## Ranking

(d) > (c) ≈ (e1) > (b) > (e2) > (a) > (f)

Firm pairs, scored for discrimination: (d) > (a), (d) > (b), (d) > (e2), (d) > (f), (c) > (a), (c) > (b), (c) > (e2), (c) > (f), (e1) > (f), (b) > (f), (e2) > (f), (a) > (f).

Contested pairs, either order accepted when argued, counted for stability only: (d) vs (c), (d) vs (e1), (c) vs (e1), (e1) vs (b), (e1) vs (a), (e1) vs (e2), (b) vs (e2), (b) vs (a), (e2) vs (a).

No source orders the programmes. The reviews settle the ends (Waters 2019; Potter et al. 2022; Potter et al. 2026) and leave the middle to the primary literature. The ordering above is the key's own, on the apparatus's axes.
