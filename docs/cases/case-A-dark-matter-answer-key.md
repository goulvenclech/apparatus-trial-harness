# Case A — Dark matter: answer key

**Why this case was chosen as the pilot.** It is the only candidate meeting all three requirements at once: several rival programmes inside a single domain, a recent consensus answer key, and direct overlap with the apparatus's own source (Branahl 2025), which explicitly discusses beyond-the-Standard-Model physics, the nature of dark matter and quantum gravity as candidates for « stagnant » status.

**The paired programmes** — same domain, documented status diverges:
- **WIMP / supersymmetry**: parameter space pushed back for decades with no detection. The LUX-ZEPLIN (LZ) experiment analysed 280 days of data (220 days between March 2023 and April 2024, plus 60 days from the first run) and found no WIMP above 9 GeV/c², a result roughly five times better than the previous world record (announced 26 August 2024). The spokesperson, Chamkaur Ghag, described the constraints as « leaving significantly fewer places for WIMPs to hide ». Supersymmetry has produced no signal at the LHC, which has sharpened the little hierarchy problem.
- **Axion**: empirically active and progressive, independently motivated as the minimal solution to the strong CP problem, with a generation of experiments (ADMX-type haloscopes, ORGAN, QUAX) beginning to probe the plausible parameter space.
- **Primordial black holes (PBH)**: a recently revived alternative; the « asteroid-mass window » (≈ 10¹⁷–10²³ g) remains open, and new work on the memory-burden effect (Thoss, Burkert & Kohri, *MNRAS* 532, 451-459, 2024) has reopened windows previously thought closed.

**External answer key** — freezable, references verified:
- Cooley, J. et al. *Report of the Topical Group on Particle Dark Matter for Snowmass 2021*, arXiv:2209.07426 (2022); Jaeckel, J., Rybka, G. & Winslow, L. *Report of the Topical Group on Wave Dark Matter for Snowmass 2021*, arXiv:2209.08125 (2022).
- Navas, S. et al. (Particle Data Group). *Review of Particle Physics*, Phys. Rev. D **110**, 030001 (2024) — « Dark Matter » and « Supersymmetry » sections.
- Branahl, J. *Stagnant Lakatosian research programmes*, European Journal for Philosophy of Science **15**, art. 53 (2025), DOI 10.1007/s13194-025-00677-x (preprint arXiv:2404.18307).

**Expected verdicts:**
- WIMP/SUSY → **stagnant** (in Branahl's sense) or **fragile**, expected pathologies **ad-hocness** (parameter space pushed back continually without positive prediction) and **invisible degeneration**. Supporting passage: the RPP 2024 records that there is at present no unambiguous experimental evidence of a breakdown of the Standard Model, and documents the extensive exclusion of WIMP parameter space.
- Axion → **defensible with reservations**, an active and progressive programme; no major pathology expected.
- PBH → **inconclusive-by-evidence** or **defensible with reservations**: several windows closed, one still open, no detection yet.

The success criterion is **discrimination**: the apparatus should rank axion > PBH > WIMP/SUSY along the progressive-to-stagnant axis, rather than issue an absolute label.

**Training-data contamination.** Moderate to high: the Snowmass reports and the RPP are in the training data. But because the status here is an *ordinal comparison*, and because the apparatus's own source (Branahl) covers this ground, the case makes an excellent first test of internal consistency.
